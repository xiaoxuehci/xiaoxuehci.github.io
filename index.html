About me

I am Xiao XUE, a design researcher working across Human-Computer Interaction, responsible AI, and Research through Design. My work examines how people encounter and shape emerging technologies in everyday life, and how design can make their assumptions and possible futures open to discussion.

I am currently a research intern at The Future Laboratory, Tsinghua University. Through participatory and designerly methods, I connect people's experiences with critical reflection and the exploration of alternatives.

I hold an MSc in Design Informatics with Distinction from the University of Edinburgh, where I developed a grounding in Research through Design alongside qualitative and quantitative research methods. My earlier training was in Internet and Multimedia Technologies at The Hong Kong Polytechnic University, where my undergraduate dissertation built a real-time facial-expression recognition system with a custom-trained convolutional neural network.

I am applying for PhD positions where I can further develop empirical and design-led research on emerging technologies, public meaning-making, and more contestable AI futures.

Research

My research is grounded in the belief that design can make technological futures discussable before they become settled infrastructure. I work with emerging systems such as large language models, smart homes, autonomous vehicles, and AI-enabled safeguarding, studying how they redistribute agency, care, responsibility, and power in everyday contexts.

I see design research as offering a pre-paradigmatic space for HCI to examine how AI systems affect people and society before technical and policy framings harden. Through situated and bottom-up methods, my work asks how the configurations and decisions around AI can become more contestable to non-technical publics and to communities marginalized or overlooked in dominant technological narratives. I am also interested in how people identify and make sense of AI-related dark patterns, and how affected communities can articulate concerns, build agency, and imagine reparative responses.

To examine these questions, I combine design research with empirical and technical approaches, including Research through Design, participatory design, system prototyping, experiments, and qualitative analysis. Across these methods, I study how people encounter, interpret, and respond to emerging AI systems in situated contexts.
