About me

I am Xiao XUE, a design researcher working in Human-Computer Interaction. I am currently a research intern at The Future Laboratory, Tsinghua University. My work uses qualitative and participatory design methods to examine people's experiences of smart homes, autonomous vehicles, generative AI, and AI safeguards.

I hold an MSc in Design Informatics with Distinction from the University of Edinburgh, where I developed my grounding in Research through Design and qualitative research. My undergraduate training was in Internet and Multimedia Technologies at The Hong Kong Polytechnic University, with a technical background in software engineering, game design, data visualization, and deep learning. For my dissertation, I built a real-time facial-expression recognition system using a custom-trained convolutional neural network.

I am applying for PhD positions in responsible AI, focusing on ethical and social issues from a sociotechnical perspective. I am particularly interested in AI-related harms and risks for non-experts and marginalized or overlooked communities, and how their participation can inform AI design and governance.

Research

I am drawn to situations where people's experiences challenge familiar judgments about AI and its users. Technologies introduced to help can create new burdens, while people whose judgment is discounted can offer important insights into what those technologies should do. I examine how system behaviour, deployment decisions, and everyday use shape these tensions, including harms and risks that may be overlooked.

I draw on qualitative, participatory, and design-led methods—including workshops, co-design, enactment, and making—to understand people's experiences and explore alternative ways of designing and using AI. I examine these possibilities from a sociotechnical perspective, considering how technical capabilities, everyday relationships, and institutional arrangements enable or constrain them. This involves ethical questions about whose concerns count, how benefits and burdens are distributed, and how affected people can participate in defining problems and evaluating responses. I aim to develop insights and design directions that inform more responsible AI development, adoption, and governance.
